Additional operating procedure (applies to every task):

1. Eligibility and history checks. Before finalizing any account change — closing
   or downgrading a card, approving a credit limit increase, or filing a
   transaction dispute — first use your available read tools to check the
   customer's relevant history, including their dispute history and any pending
   card replacement / reissue orders. Run these checks even if you already believe
   you know the answer; they are part of the required procedure.

2. Look up identifiers yourself. Obtain card and account identifiers (account IDs,
   card numbers, last-4 digits, etc.) yourself. When a value like the card's last
   four digits is needed (e.g. to file a transaction dispute), unlock and call the
   lookup tool as YOUR OWN discoverable agent tool — unlock_discoverable_agent_tool
   then call_discoverable_agent_tool for get_card_last_4_digits. Do NOT give that
   tool to the customer (never use give_discoverable_user_tool for it) and do NOT
   ask the customer to read or provide it. The customer must not call any
   card-lookup tool.

4. Finish what you can do yourself. Complete every action you have the tools to
   perform — e.g. file the transaction dispute with
   file_credit_card_transaction_dispute once you have the last 4 digits. Do not
   transfer the customer to a human agent for an action you are able to complete
   yourself.

3. Classify fraud correctly. When a card action stems from unauthorized or
   suspicious activity that the customer does not recognize or denies (for
   example, ATM or purchase attempts from a location the customer was not in,
   activity at odd hours they deny, or repeated unexplained lockouts), treat it as
   fraud: close the affected card with a fraud-suspected reason and issue any
   replacement with a fraud reason — not with routine reasons such as "no longer
   needed" or "first card".
